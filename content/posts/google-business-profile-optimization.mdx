**Google Business Profile optimization** is one of the highest-impact things a local service business can do. Your profile appears in Google Search and Google Maps, often above every website, and it's frequently the first impression a customer has of your business. Many customers call straight from the profile without ever visiting your website.

This guide walks through every part of your profile, explains what matters most for ranking in the map pack and shows how to keep your profile working week after week.

## How Google ranks local businesses

Google describes three main factors for local results: **relevance** (how well your profile matches the search), **distance** (how close you are to the searcher or the area they searched) and **prominence** (how well known and trusted your business is). You can't move your location, but you can strongly influence relevance and prominence, and that's what optimization is about.

## Step 1: Claim, verify and protect your profile

If you haven't claimed your profile, do it first and complete verification. Then check who has access. Remove former employees and agencies you no longer work with, and make sure at least two people you trust have owner or manager access so you're never locked out.

## Step 2: Choose the right categories

Your primary category is one of the most important ranking signals you control.

### Pick the most specific primary category

Choose the category that best describes your main service. "HVAC contractor" is better than "Contractor"; "Roofing contractor" is better than "Construction company". Look at which categories the top-ranking competitors in your area use for inspiration, but only choose categories that genuinely describe you.

### Add relevant secondary categories

Add secondary categories for other services you really offer, such as "Water heater installation service" for a plumber. Don't add categories for services you don't provide; it confuses customers and can hurt trust.

## Step 3: Complete every business detail

A complete profile is more useful to customers and gives Google more to work with.

### Name, address and phone

Use your real business name with no extra keywords. Adding keywords to your name breaks Google's guidelines and can lead to suspension. Keep your address and phone number identical to your website.

### Service areas and hours

If you travel to customers, set the towns or regions you serve. Keep hours accurate, including special hours for holidays. Few things frustrate customers more than calling a business that's listed as open but isn't.

### Services and descriptions

List each service with a short description. These help your profile match more specific searches. Write your business description in plain language: what you do, where you do it and why customers choose you.

### Attributes and links

Add attributes that apply, such as online estimates or emergency service, and add links for booking or quotes.

## Step 4: Post every week

Google Business Profile posts let you share updates, offers and recent work directly on your profile. Regular posts show customers and Google that your business is active.

### What to post

- Recently completed jobs with photos and the neighborhood
- Seasonal tips, such as preparing a furnace for winter
- Limited-time offers and maintenance plan reminders
- Answers to common questions

### How often

Once a week is a strong, sustainable rhythm. Consistency over months matters more than a burst of posts followed by silence.

## Step 5: Add real photos consistently

Photos help customers decide and can increase engagement with your profile. Upload photos of your team, your vehicles, your workspace and especially finished jobs. Add a few new photos every month rather than a big batch once a year. Avoid stock images; customers want to see the people who'll come to their home.

## Step 6: Build a steady stream of reviews

Reviews influence both ranking and the customer's decision. Google considers the number, recency and quality of reviews.

### Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is right after a job goes well, while the customer is happy. Send a short text or email with a direct link to your review form.

### Never buy or fake reviews

Fake reviews violate Google's policies and can result in removed reviews or profile restrictions. They also break the trust that makes reviews valuable in the first place.

### Reply to every review

Thank positive reviewers personally and mention the service you performed. For negative reviews, stay calm, acknowledge the issue, avoid arguing in public and invite the customer to contact you directly. Future customers read your replies to judge how you'll treat them.

## Step 7: Use questions and answers

Customers can ask questions on your profile. Monitor them and answer quickly. You can also add common questions yourself and answer them, which helps customers find information fast.

## Step 8: Keep everything consistent with your website

Your profile should link to the most relevant page on your website, and your website should show the same name, address, phone number and hours. Consistency between your profile, website and directories reinforces trust. ## Step 9: Track what's working

Your profile shows calls, website clicks, direction requests and the searches that triggered your listing. Review these monthly. If calls rise after you start weekly posts, you know it's working. If a service gets searches but few clicks, improve its description and photos.

Rankings also vary across your service area. Geo-grid tracking shows where you appear in the map pack from many points around town, which is far more useful than checking from your own office.

## A simple weekly profile routine

Optimization isn't a one-time project. This 20-minute weekly routine keeps a profile active and accurate:

1. **Monday:** publish one post about a recent job, a seasonal tip or an offer.
2. **Tuesday:** upload two or three new photos from the past week.
3. **Wednesday:** send review requests to every customer whose job was completed.
4. **Thursday:** reply to all new reviews and answer any new questions.
5. **Friday:** check hours for upcoming holidays and skim your profile for unwanted edits.

Google allows the public to suggest changes to business details, so a quick weekly check helps you catch incorrect edits before they cost you calls. Once a month, review your performance data and update your services list if you have added or stopped offering anything.

## Common Google Business Profile mistakes

- Stuffing keywords into the business name
- Using a virtual office address that breaks guidelines
- Letting the profile go quiet for months
- Ignoring negative reviews or replying angrily
- Inconsistent phone numbers across the web
- Choosing broad categories instead of specific ones
