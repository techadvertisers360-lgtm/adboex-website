An **AI receptionist for small business** answers your phone calls and website chats automatically, around the clock, using conversational AI. It greets callers, answers common questions, collects details and books appointments, then sends the information to you. For local service businesses, where every missed call can be a missed job, it closes one of the most expensive gaps in the customer journey.

This guide explains how AI receptionists work, what they can and can't do, how they compare to other options and how to set one up well.

## Why missed calls cost small businesses so much

When someone needs a plumber, a locksmith or an HVAC technician, they often call several businesses until one answers. If you're on a job, with another customer or closed for the night, that call goes to voicemail, and many callers won't leave a message. They simply call the next company.

For many small businesses the cost isn't one lost job. It's the lost customer lifetime value: the repeat service, the maintenance plan and the referrals that would have followed. All the effort you put into [local SEO](/insights/local-seo-checklist-for-service-businesses/) and your [Google Business Profile](/insights/google-business-profile-optimization/) only pays off if someone answers when the phone rings.

## What is an AI receptionist?

An AI receptionist is software that handles conversations with customers using natural language. Modern systems combine speech recognition, a language model and your business information to hold a natural conversation by phone or chat.

### What it typically does

- Answers calls and chats 24 hours a day, 7 days a week
- Greets callers in your business's name and tone
- Answers questions about services, hours, service areas and pricing guidance
- Collects the caller's name, phone number, address and the details of their request
- Books appointments into your calendar or sends requests for confirmation
- Identifies urgent situations and alerts you immediately
- Sends you a summary of every conversation

### What it shouldn't do

An AI receptionist shouldn't invent prices, promise things your team can't deliver or handle sensitive situations that need a human. A good setup gives it clear information and clear rules for when to hand off to a person.

## How an AI receptionist works, step by step

### 1. The call or chat comes in

Calls can be forwarded to the AI at all times, only after hours or only when your team doesn't pick up within a few rings. Website chats connect directly.

### 2. The AI understands the request

The caller speaks naturally: "My air conditioner is blowing warm air and it's 95 degrees." The AI identifies the service needed and how urgent it is.

### 3. It asks the right qualifying questions

Based on your setup, it asks for the address, the type of system, how long the problem has been happening or whatever details your team needs to prepare.

### 4. It books or routes the request

The AI books an available slot, sends an urgent alert to your on-call technician or captures the request for a callback, depending on the rules you set.

### 5. You get a summary

You receive the caller's details and a short summary by text or in your dashboard, so you can follow up with full context.

## AI receptionist vs. other options

### Voicemail

Free, but many callers hang up without leaving a message, and those who do still wait for a callback.

### Answering services with human operators

Human answering services can be excellent, but costs usually rise with call volume, and operators may only take messages rather than book appointments.

### Hiring a full-time receptionist

A great in-house receptionist is valuable, but covering evenings, weekends and holidays is expensive, and one person can only handle one call at a time.

### AI receptionist

Available at all hours, handles multiple conversations at once and books directly, typically for a predictable monthly cost. Many businesses use AI for overflow and after-hours calls while their team answers during the day.

## What does an AI receptionist cost?

Pricing models vary, so compare them on the total cost of handling your real call volume rather than the headline price.

- **Per-minute pricing** charges for the time the AI spends on calls. It can be affordable at low volume but becomes unpredictable during busy seasons.
- **Per-call or per-lead pricing** charges for each conversation or captured lead. Simple to understand, but costs rise exactly when business is good.
- **Flat monthly pricing** covers a set level of usage for a fixed fee, which makes budgeting easier for seasonal businesses.
- **Bundled pricing** includes the receptionist inside a wider platform, alongside tools you may already pay for separately, such as review management or reporting.

To judge value, estimate how many calls you miss each month, multiply by how often those callers would have become customers and by your average job value. Even a few recovered jobs each month often covers the cost of the service. Also check setup fees, contract length and whether call recordings and transcripts are included.

## What to look for in an AI receptionist

- **Natural conversation.** Callers should feel helped, not trapped in a phone menu.
- **Your business knowledge.** It should be easy to add your services, service areas, hours and policies.
- **Calendar booking.** Booking directly saves a round of phone tag.
- **Urgent call handling.** Emergencies should reach a human immediately.
- **Phone and website chat.** Customers contact you both ways.
- **Clear summaries.** You should know exactly what was said.
- **Data privacy.** Choose a provider that doesn't sell or share your leads or customer data.

## How to set up an AI receptionist the right way

### Write down your most common questions

List the 15 to 20 questions your team answers every week: prices, areas served, hours, emergency availability, financing and warranties. Give the AI clear, approved answers.

### Define what counts as urgent

Decide which situations should trigger an immediate alert, such as no heat in winter, active leaks or a car that won't start, and who should receive that alert.

### Set booking rules

Decide which appointment types the AI can book directly and which need confirmation, and connect your calendar to avoid double bookings.

### Test it like a customer

Call it yourself. Ask awkward questions. Try an emergency scenario. Adjust the answers until it sounds like your best team member on a good day.

### Review conversations weekly at first

Read summaries for the first few weeks. You'll quickly spot questions to add and answers to improve.

## Will customers mind talking to AI?

Most customers care about getting help quickly. A clear, friendly AI that answers instantly and books them in is usually a better experience than voicemail. Being transparent helps: the AI can introduce itself as your virtual assistant, and callers who want a person can ask to be connected or receive a callback.
